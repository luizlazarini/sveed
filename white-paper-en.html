TECHNICAL WHITE PAPER

Airplanes, Rockets and Trains: Why Traditional EdTech Fails in Brazilian Public Education Policies

And How Deterministic Infrastructure Solves the Auditability Gap

Version 1.1 | January 2026
Authors: SVEED Team
INPI Registration: BR 51 2026 000328-8

Executive Summary

The massive distribution of educational devices in Brazilian public policies faces a critical paradox: evidence of physical delivery does not equal evidence of functional use. While invoices, delivery terms and logistics records prove the acquisition and distribution of technological assets, such instruments are insufficient to demonstrate their operational execution in the educational context.

As the Federal Court of Accounts (TCU) and the Office of the Comptroller General (CGU) intensify audits on the effectiveness of investments in educational technology, public managers, suppliers and integrators lack technical instruments capable of proving, in an objective and auditable way, the functional use of contracted devices.

This document analyzes why traditional EdTech solutions — designed for high-connectivity environments, updated hardware and robust infrastructure — systematically fail when applied to the Brazilian reality, characterized by intermittent connectivity, legacy hardware and strict compliance requirements with the General Data Protection Law (LGPD).

The concept of deterministic verification infrastructure is presented as a viable and necessary technical alternative, demonstrating how offline-first architectures, agnostic to pedagogical content and based on local processing solve the auditability problem without compromising user privacy or depending on ideal operating conditions.

Key Conclusions

First conclusion. Global EdTech solutions (such as GoGuardian, Lightspeed, Securly and corporate MDMs) were designed for controlled and highly connected environments — the "airplanes and rockets" — and do not operate effectively in the "alleys and dense forests" of Brazilian public education.

Second conclusion. The auditability gap identified by the TCU (Rulings No. 326/2022 and No. 286/2025) remains without adequate technical response in the market.

Third conclusion. Deterministic verification infrastructure provides objective evidence of functional use, without collecting personal data, aligning with the regulatory, operational and legal requirements of the public sector.

1. The Problem: Evidence of Delivery ≠ Evidence of Use

1.1 Context of Public Policies for Educational Technology

Since the launch of the National Educational Technology Program (ProInfo) in 1997, the Brazilian federal government has invested significant resources in the acquisition and distribution of technological devices to public schools. Between 2012 and 2015, the National Education Development Fund (FNDE) coordinated the acquisition of approximately 600,000 educational tablets intended for high school teachers.

Despite the significant volume of investments, audits conducted by control agencies identified critical gaps in proving the effective use of these assets. The TCU, in an operational audit conducted in 2022 (Ruling No. 326/2022-TCU-Plenary), pointed out the risk of misalignment between the acquisition of ICT and the requirements of public digital education policies, compromising the effectiveness of the investments made.

Ruling No. 286/2025-TCU-Plenary reinforced this diagnosis by highlighting the need for mechanisms capable of proving not only physical delivery, but the functional execution of devices in the educational context.

1.2 The Operational Verification Challenge

The central problem can be summarized as follows:

Invoice + Delivery Term ≠ Evidence of Functional Use

Public managers are confronted with recurring questions:

  • Are the devices being used by the final beneficiaries (students and teachers)?
  • Does use occur with minimum regularity compatible with the objectives of public policy?
  • Is it possible to prove operational execution without violating privacy rights?
  • How to audit use in low-connectivity contexts, where real-time reporting is unfeasible?

For suppliers and operators, the absence of objective evidence of functional use represents contractual and reputational risk, potentially resulting in questions from control agencies, payment retention and losses in future bids.

1.3 Operational Context Data

The Brazilian educational infrastructure imposes technical restrictions that make the direct adoption of conventional EdTech solutions unfeasible:

Connectivity

According to the 2023 School Census (INEP), about 35% of public schools do not have adequate internet access or have intermittent connectivity. In rural areas and urban peripheries, this percentage exceeds 50%.

Hardware

A large part of the distributed devices run Android 7 or earlier versions, with 1.5 GB to 2 GB of RAM. Hardware update cycles range from five to seven years, incompatible with the requirements of modern SaaS platforms.

Regulation and Privacy

The LGPD (Law No. 13,709/2018) imposes severe restrictions on the collection and processing of personal data of minors. Solutions based on monitoring content, behavior or individual communications face significant legal barriers in the context of public education.

2. Airplanes and Rockets: Why Global EdTech Doesn't Solve the Problem

2.1 Behavioral Monitoring Platforms

Platforms such as GoGuardian, Lightspeed Systems, Securly and Gaggle were developed to monitor student activity in highly connected school environments, focusing on digital security and behavioral surveillance.

These solutions depend on continuous connectivity, perform extensive personal data collection and prioritize content monitoring, not operational verification. In low-connectivity contexts, they become inoperative; from the perspective of LGPD, they present structural legal risks; and, from the perspective of public audit, they do not answer the central question: is the device being functionally used for educational purposes?

2.2 Corporate Mobile Device Management (MDM)

MDM tools were designed for property management and security of corporate fleets. Although useful for inventory and device control, they do not verify the execution of specific educational activities, nor do they distinguish educational use from recreational use.

Time-of-use reports produced by MDM systems indicate only that the device was turned on, not that educational routines were executed. For public audit purposes, such data is insufficient to demonstrate functional effectiveness.

2.3 Educational Platforms with Analytics

Platforms such as Google Classroom, Moodle and Canvas offer detailed usage reports and measure student engagement in specific content.

However, these platforms require continuous connectivity and are limited to contexts where access to the internet is reliable. In low-connectivity contexts or with heavy use of offline apps, such platforms cannot provide data on effective use.

Furthermore, engagement metrics (logins, page views, task submissions) measure interaction, not functional execution — the execution of defined educational routines with minimum regularity.

2.4 The Structural Bias of Global Solutions

The common characteristic of these solutions is that they were designed for contexts very different from the Brazilian reality:

  • High bandwidth and constant connectivity
  • Updated hardware and frequent replacement cycles
  • Permissive regulations regarding data collection
  • IT infrastructure and specialized technical support

Attempting to apply "airplanes and rockets" to the "alleys and dense forests" of Brazilian public education results in operational, legal and financial failure.

3. Rails and Trains: Deterministic Verification Infrastructure

3.1 Redefining the Problem

The technical challenge is not to monitor content or behavior. The challenge is to prove, at scale, offline and in accordance with the LGPD, that functional educational routines were executed.

Deterministic verification infrastructure is designed precisely for this context: reliable, auditable, simple and compatible with operational, legal and technological limitations.

3.2 The Six Pillars of Deterministic Architecture

Pillar 1: Offline-First with Asynchronous Synchronization

The system operates completely offline. Synchronization with central servers occurs only when Wi-Fi is available, without depending on connectivity to function. Data is stored locally and synchronized in batches when connectivity is restored.

Pillar 2: Monotonic Timer for Temporal Integrity

Use of Android's monotonic timer (SystemClock.elapsedRealtime) ensures that time measurements are not subject to manipulation by changes to the system clock. This guarantees the integrity of temporal records.

Pillar 3: Temporal Gatekeeper

The system only operates outside school hours and on school days. This design ensures that educational routines are executed in the appropriate context, without interfering with regular class activities.

Pillar 4: Operational Nexus Detection with Human Confirmation

The system uses multiple checkpoints (audio orientation, execution pause, interactive confirmation) to ensure that a human is actively executing the routine. This prevents false positives and ensures functional use.

Pillar 5: Regional Aggregation with SHA-256 Hash

Data is aggregated by region (identified by MCC/MNC) and protected by SHA-256 hash. Individual disaggregation is technically impossible, ensuring compliance with LGPD by design.

Pillar 6: MCC/MNC Geolocation (Without GPS)

Identification of the geographical region is done by the Mobile Country Code (MCC) and Mobile Network Code (MNC) of the SIM card, without using GPS or precise location. This allows regional distribution of data without compromising individual privacy.

3.3 The Five-Step Operational Flow

Each educational session consists of five sequential stages:

  1. Audio Orientation: Clear guidance on the subject area to be worked on.
  2. Review Block: Quick review of previous content to ensure continuity.
  3. Mandatory Pause: Short pause for rest, preventing fatigue.
  4. Execution Block with Checkpoints: Main activity with periodic confirmations of human presence.
  5. Closure: Completion of session, local recording and device release.

At the end of the cycle, the device is released for free use (games, social networks, general applications), without restrictions.

4. Evidence of Unmet Demand

4.1 TCU Audits

The Ruling No. 326/2022-TCU-Plenary pointed to the risk of misalignment between the acquisition of ICT and the requirements of public digital education policies. The Ruling No. 286/2025-TCU-Plenary reinforced the need for mechanisms capable of proving the functional execution of devices.

4.2 FNDE Data and CETIC.br TIC Education

FNDE data and surveys conducted by CETIC.br (Brazilian Internet Steering Committee) indicate that a significant portion of distributed devices is underutilized or inactive due to lack of adequate mechanisms for operational verification.

4.3 Market Gap

Currently, there is no solution on the market that simultaneously meets the following requirements:

  • Operation in offline contexts
  • Structural compliance with LGPD (no personal data collection)
  • Objective proof of functional use for audit purposes
  • Compatibility with legacy hardware (Android 7+, 1.5 GB RAM)

5. Rails Already Exist: The SVEED Case

5.1 INPI Registration

SVEED (Verification of Educational Use Routines) is a computer program with software registration granted by the National Institute of Industrial Property (INPI), process BR 51 2026 000328-8, published in RPI 2873 of 01/27/2026.

Legal nature: technical infrastructure software for public governance, valid for 50 years from the date of creation.

5.2 Technical Specifications

  • Minimum Requirements: Android 7.0+, 1.5 GB RAM
  • Application Size: ~7.5 MB
  • Local Database: Room/SQLite with 5-day sliding window
  • Synchronization: Asynchronous via Wi-Fi when available
  • Offline Operation: Does not depend on internet connection

5.3 Functional Nature

SVEED is GovTech infrastructure, not EdTech. It does not teach content, does not evaluate pedagogical quality, does not infer learning. Its function is exclusively to verify that educational routines were executed functionally.

5.4 Technical Validation

The Deterministic Protocol Validation Report is being conducted by an independent company, certifying the technical accuracy and operational integrity of the architecture.

6. Implications for Stakeholders

6.1 Public Managers

Deterministic verification infrastructure reduces audit risk and provides objective evidence of functional use without violating privacy. It aligns with the principles of efficiency, economy and legality required by Art. 70 of the Federal Constitution.

6.2 Suppliers and Integrators

For suppliers, offering deterministic verification infrastructure as part of the technological solution represents contractual and reputational protection. In bids, inclusion of objective verification clauses can be a decisive competitive differentiator.

6.3 Control Agencies

For TCU, CGU and state courts of accounts, deterministic infrastructure provides aggregated data, auditable and compliant with LGPD, enabling objective evaluation of the effectiveness of investments in educational technology.

6.4 Operators

Telecommunications operators can offer deterministic verification infrastructure as added value in public contracts, differentiating themselves in bids and strengthening relationships with the public sector.

7. Adoption Roadmap

Phase 1: Municipal Proof of Concept (90 Days)

  • Pilot test in a single municipality with 1,000 to 3,000 devices
  • Technical, legal and operational validation
  • Audit reports and proof of concept documentation

Phase 2: Regional Scale (180 Days)

  • Expansion to state level or multiple municipalities
  • Validation of synchronization and aggregation mechanisms
  • Refinement of operational metrics

Phase 3: National Adoption

  • Inclusion of functional verification requirements in public notice specifications
  • Adoption of deterministic infrastructure as good practice
  • Compliance with economy principles (Art. 70, CF/88)

8. Conclusion

Airplanes and rockets were designed for controlled, predictable and highly connected environments. Rails and trains were designed for contexts where reliability, simplicity and scalability are more important than speed.

Brazilian public education needs "rails and trains": simple, auditable, offline-compatible and privacy-respecting infrastructure that proves functional use without depending on ideal conditions.

The auditability gap identified by the TCU remains without adequate response in the market. Deterministic verification infrastructure provides this answer.

Final Message

Airplanes and rockets need runways and sky. Rails and trains just need to exist. And the rails already exist.
