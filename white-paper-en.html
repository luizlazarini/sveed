School Review for Public Education Across the Global South

Conceived for public-school children across the Global South, SVEED starts in Brazil with particular attention to families in its C, D and E socioeconomic groups. Its Android home review uses exercises and hints with less reliance on continuous connectivity or adult explanations.

Executive summary

Supporting public education after class

SVEED was conceived for children in public education across the Global South, beginning with Brazilian families in the C, D and E socioeconomic groups. Its purpose is to expand access to home review of content already taught at school, including where connectivity and adult study support are limited.

Brazil’s BNCC is the first national curriculum implementation. Adapting the system elsewhere would require translation, country-specific curriculum work, independent educational review and local institutional validation. SVEED does not yet operate outside Brazil or have a formal school pilot; educational effectiveness remains to be independently evaluated.

Schools teach in groups, but much of practice, review, and consolidation happens individually after class. Homework and other forms of at-home review have long helped serve that function. The challenge is that the conditions required to continue learning at home are not equally available to every child.

International organizations use the concept of the last mile across several dimensions of education: the OECD discusses connectivity reaching students' homes and personal devices; UNICEF refers to last-mile learners; and the World Bank uses the term last-mile families when addressing access to learning materials at home.

Source: OECD 2023 ↗  ·  Source: UNICEF 2022 ↗  ·  Source: World Bank — Read@Home ↗

Schools teach. SVEED makes review possible. Educational assessments measure proficiency.

SVEED is an Android system designed to make structured, regular, independent review at home possible, with the educational aim of helping children consolidate what they have learned. It provides exercises and support when difficulties arise, reducing the need for an adult to explain content already taught at school.

Contributing to learning is its educational aim. Its operational role is to make review possible; it is not designed to measure or certify proficiency. SVEED’s specific effects on study regularity and learning have not yet been measured systematically. Its architecture also includes activity records and components already implemented for Educational Use Verification in device programmes. This institutional foundation, described in Chapter 12, provides an opportunity for an operating co-founder to lead integration and deployment.

1 · The last mile

The last mile extends beyond the school building

“Last mile” is not a term invented by SVEED. The OECD devotes a section of its Digital Education Outlook 2023 to the last mile problem in digital education and notes that this last mile can extend from schools into students' homes and personal devices. Source: OECD 2023 ↗

UNICEF uses the term last-mile learners for children who are being left behind and are among the hardest to reach. UNESCO’s report highlights equitable learning and the importance of investing in teachers and reforms that expand access to quality education. Source: UNICEF 2022 ↗   Source: UNESCO — EFA Global Monitoring Report 2013/4 (official report page) ↗

At-home review has long served part of this function

The need for continuity after class is not new. Homework is a long-standing instructional practice that can extend classroom concepts, support self-regulation, and contribute to learning. OECD evidence also points to the importance of task design and indicates that short, regular assignments can provide benefits without unnecessarily widening inequities. Source: PISA 2022 ↗

Educational rationale: curriculum-based practice and homework

The Education Endowment Foundation’s (EEF) Homework evidence review reports positive average effects, with larger effects in secondary than in primary education. It emphasizes tasks connected to classroom learning, feedback, and quality rather than quantity. The EEF rates the security of this evidence as low; outcomes vary across settings and educational stages. Source: EEF — Homework ↗

This research supports the educational rationale for offering curriculum-based practice with guidance. Applying these principles to SVEED is a design hypothesis to be evaluated: effects found in other interventions do not automatically apply to this app. The cited sources do not constitute an evaluation or endorsement of SVEED.

The challenge is that the conditions needed to complete that review are not equally distributed. PISA 2018 links lower homework participation among disadvantaged students to factors such as lack of a quiet place to study, unequal access to internet and computers, and less available parental support. In its Brazil-specific analysis, the OECD also documents substantial inequalities in access to learning resources at home. Source: PISA 2018 ↗   Source: OECD — Education in Brazil, Chapter 6, Figure 6.7 (PISA 2018) ↗

The World Bank addresses a related dimension through Read@Home, an initiative designed to bring reading and learning materials into households and reach vulnerable families with limited access to those resources. Source: World Bank — Read@Home ↗

SVEED does not begin from the assumption that schools, teachers, or families have “failed.” It begins from a structural reality: learning continuity after class depends on conditions that vary widely across students. Its purpose is to reduce some of those dependencies through a simple, guided review system.

2 · Scope

Reviewing taught content without replacing schools, teachers, or the curriculum

SVEED aims to make review manageable for children: practicing skills already taught at school, receiving hints and feedback, revisiting prerequisites when needed, and maintaining a study routine. These mechanisms serve the aim of consolidating learning; their effects need to be evaluated in use.

SVEED does not claim to solve education's entire last-mile problem on its own. It does not provide internet access, distribute devices, or replace public policy. It was designed to reduce concrete barriers: dependence on continuous connectivity, constant adult guidance, and the break between classroom instruction and individual review at home.

What SVEED does

  • reviews curriculum skills that have already been taught;
  • generates exercises locally from rules and templates;
  • offers progressive hints when available;
  • provides supporting concepts separately from hints;
  • temporarily reduces difficulty when the child struggles;
  • returns to the target skill once the child is ready to return.

Deliberate limits

  • does not replace a teacher or classroom instruction;
  • should not autonomously introduce new curriculum content;
  • does not identify the underlying reason for each incorrect answer;
  • does not use points, coins, rankings, or streaks to drive the session;
  • does not depend on generative AI to create questions for the child.

Curriculum coverage and demonstration sample

SVEED — Sistema de Viabilização de Estudo Estruturado Domiciliar (a system for enabling structured study at home) — has a Brazilian curriculum structure covering Years 1–9 of Brazil’s Ensino Fundamental (primary and lower secondary education): Mathematics, Portuguese, Science, History, and Geography across those years, and English in Years 6–9. The existing curriculum materials are at different stages of specialist educational review.

The evaluation APK provides Mathematics for Years 2 and 7 as a demonstration sample. It allows reviewers to examine this content and the review workflow; it neither limits the scope of the system nor demonstrates consistent quality across the entire collection.

3 · Pedagogical flow

Error, hint, retry, and continuity

Target skill

The system starts from a skill previously identified by the child or through teacher mode.

Practice exercise

The child works through an exercise generated on the device within a defined set of curriculum content.

First error

When a hint is available, SVEED offers support without immediately turning the error into visual punishment.

Second chance

The child can use the hint and try again. If another error occurs, the system provides the feedback needed to continue.

Hints may exist in up to three levels, depending on the available content. The Concept Library is a different layer: while a hint guides reasoning within that exercise, the library explains the related concept.

Error is not treated merely as a score. It acts as a signal to adjust the experience, provide support, and preserve continuity in the review.

4 · Rule-based adaptation

The orbit: adjusting difficulty and revisiting prerequisites

Review works around a target skill with limited local variation. The orbit consists of TARGET, TARGET-1, TARGET-2, and TARGET+1.

TARGETThe main skill the child needs to review.
TARGET-1A temporary reduction in complexity to maintain flow and reduce cognitive load.
TARGET-2A second reduction step when difficulty persists.
TARGET+1A controlled probe above the target. If difficulty appears, the system quickly returns.

Moving down does not mean abandoning the objective. It can work as cognitive breathing room: the child returns to a relatively simpler range, interrupts a frustrating sequence of errors, and then returns to the target.

When difficulty persists, the curriculum graph can be used to step back to an associated prerequisite. The graph is used only downward; it is not a mechanism for pushing the child into new curriculum progressions.

Operational formulation: when successive errors indicate difficulty moving forward, SVEED can step back, through predefined rules, to an associated curriculum prerequisite and then guide the student back to the original path.

5 · Child experience

Simple on the surface, complex only where necessary

The child does not need to understand the orbit, graph, templates, or recovery rules. The child follows a predictable sequence with a small number of controls and help when needed.

Independent participation

Independence means being able to complete review activities with less educational support from an adult, including help with reading instructions and working through difficulties. It does not assume that children will start spontaneously: a parent or caregiver may prompt them to begin and organize the routine. The aim is to reduce the need for that adult to explain and supervise every exercise.

Text-to-speech to support independent use

The operating system's text-to-speech can read prompts and instructions. For children with lower reading fluency, this reduces dependence on an adult reader. Older, fluent students can simply ignore the audio.

No gamification and no scoreboard during review

SVEED does not use coins, rankings, avatars, or artificial rewards to drive engagement. It also avoids continuously displaying a score of correct and incorrect answers during the session. The feedback shown is what is necessary to conduct the review.

6 · Routine and focus

A short, predictable session with a safe exit

The core of the product is a daily review session lasting 15 minutes, regardless of how quickly the child answers. The duration is an operational choice intended to make the routine short and predictable, not an established optimum for every age group and subject.

During review, SVEED can activate a focus mode that reduces impulsive switching to competing apps. This is not an absolute lock: incoming calls remain available, and an emergency exit allows the session to end when necessary.

The routine also includes a limited number of prompts throughout the day and a daily cutoff time, avoiding pressure on the child to complete the activity at any hour of the night.

7 · Engineering

Local content, explicit rules, and low infrastructure dependence

The application is designed to operate locally on Android. Curriculum content, prerequisite graphs, templates, and rules are embedded on the device.

Offline-first

The educational session does not depend on continuous connectivity.

Local generation

Questions are produced from rules and templates, with controlled variation and no calls to generative models.

Explicit pedagogical logic

Orbit, return to target, hints, and prerequisite recovery follow rules defined in the product.

Low technical cost per additional session

The architecture reduces network use, backend dependence, and compute cost per session.

The pedagogical logic is rule-based and auditable, but variation generation uses local randomness. For this reason, this version does not describe the system as exact reproduction or full determinism for each exercise.

Curriculum content governance. SVEED organizes skills referenced to Brazil’s BNCC curriculum and links them to exercise templates and on-device generators. Development includes internal content reviews and automated checks of structure and consistency, covering template variables, the construction of answer choices, and answer checks for the cases covered by the tests. The depth of review varies across subjects and year groups. These internal quality controls do not replace independent educational validation.

8 · Data and security

Data minimization, with limits described explicitly

SVEED does not require the child's name, national ID number, email, or phone number to run the review. The architecture does, however, use persistent technical identifiers and session records. The accurate description is therefore minimization and pseudonymization, not absolute anonymization.

Current records can track elements such as time, subject, skill, correct answers, errors, and hint use at the session level. They do not currently reconstruct the full event chain of every individual exercise.

The product should not be described as “tamper-proof,” “absolutely anonymous,” or as providing “guaranteed cryptographic integrity” until the corresponding mechanisms are effectively connected and validated in the production flow.

9 · School integration

Teacher mode: the school defines the target; SVEED conducts the review

Teacher mode allows dates and curriculum skill codes to be entered to guide review at home. The objective is to preserve alignment with what is being worked on in class.

In the product's current state, the input accepts more than one skill in the plan, but daily execution still uses the first valid skill for that day. This limitation is described here to avoid making a promise beyond the current implementation.

Teacher → review → session record. The selected skill guides the activity at home, and the session record allows its completion to be tracked. This connection can support continuity between classroom learning and review at home. The record does not certify proficiency or establish, on its own, that a particular lesson was taught.

11 · Evidence

The next step is external evaluation

Three levels of evidence need to be considered separately:

  1. Educational rationale: research on homework supports the approach of curriculum-based practice with feedback, with variable effects and limitations described in the educational rationale.
  2. Operational feasibility: the report of family use is an initial indication that children can complete sessions independently. External evaluation should examine session completion, the need for adult intervention, hint use, and continuity of the routine in other families.
  3. Specific effects: it remains necessary to measure whether, to what extent, and under what conditions SVEED contributes to regular review and learning, using suitable measures and comparisons that allow its contribution to be assessed.

The product’s hypothesis is that reducing dependence on adult explanations makes regular review easier, increasing opportunities for practice that may help consolidate learning. This proposed sequence guides evaluation; it is not yet a demonstrated result. Session records provide evidence of activity, subject to their integrity, rather than evidence of learning gains.

The strategy is to seek external guidance, including through the Learning Cabinet’s Quick Assessment, and find educational, technical, and operational partners to complete specialist review, run a documented pilot, and prepare implementation. The initial submission seeks feedback on suitability and gaps; it does not assume approval or a full assessment.

Simple for the child to use. Complex only where necessary: in the logic that keeps review appropriate, continuous, and less frustrating.

12 · Scalability and governance

From review to Educational Use Verification

SVEED connects curriculum-based review with the educational use of devices. For children, it provides a structured review routine that works offline. For device-distribution programmes, its architecture already includes activity records and components designed to verify that use. An operating co-founder will build on this foundation, leading its integration and institutional deployment. Its institutional purpose is to track how many devices allocated to a programme have actually recorded curriculum-based review, by school, grade, and class.

The existing foundation includes local session records and school context, data preparation for transmission, a synchronization queue, and cryptographic functions. Synchronization components remain disabled in the current configuration. Session closure uses a local hash; the additional HMAC and Android Keystore functions still need to be integrated and validated within the institutional protocol. This implemented work provides the starting point for the next stage.

Putting this application into operation requires completing integration with an institutional service, linking each installation to a device in the programme inventory, counting distinct devices over the reporting period, and verifying record integrity. The current identifier represents an installation; the existing daily token alone does not support counting distinct devices across days. The protocol must select the minimum activity data needed and define how they are aggregated, while protecting children’s privacy. Sending records later is compatible with offline review.

The GovTech application offers a potential distribution channel for the educational service through education authorities, systems integrators, device suppliers, and programme funders. The operating co-founder will lead integration, governance, validation with prospective contracting organisations, and deployment, alongside ongoing specialist content review and evaluation with users. Evidence of activity complements device-delivery documentation; its institutional use will depend on validation and each programme’s requirements. Learning outcomes remain subject to separate assessment.
