The problem

Teaching happens in groups

A teacher leads an entire class. Even after a good lesson, each child leaves with different questions, forgotten steps, and review needs.

Review is individual

After class, a child needs to reorganize what was learned, practice, and recover forgotten steps. That process happens at each student's own pace.

Support at home is uneven

Not every child has an adult available every day with the time and knowledge to guide review. SVEED is designed to operate in that gap.

What SVEED is

SVEED is a guided individual curriculum review infrastructure for Android. It reviews skills already covered at school, offers progressive support when a child encounters difficulty, and can recover prerequisites before guiding the child back to the target skill.

Review

The starting point is a curriculum skill already covered at school, identified by the child or selected through teacher mode.

Progressive support

After an incorrect answer, the child can receive a hint and try again. Support guides the reasoning process without turning the session into a performance scoreboard.

Prerequisites

When difficulty persists, predefined rules can step back to an associated earlier skill and then return the child to the original learning path.

Product characteristics

  • Offline-first: the educational session does not depend on continuous connectivity
  • Android: content, rules, graphs, and templates remain available locally
  • No generative AI during the session: questions are produced from local rules and templates
  • No gamification: no coins, rankings, streaks, or persistent performance scoreboard during review

School teaches. SVEED supports the review. The product is not intended to replace teachers, classroom instruction, or the curriculum, and it should not autonomously introduce curriculum content that has not yet been taught.

How it works

A short, predictable, individual routine designed to help the child keep going when difficulty appears.

The core of SVEED is a daily review session of approximately 15 minutes. The interface stays simple; the complexity remains in the logic that organizes the sequence, offers support, and adjusts difficulty.

1

Confirm what was studied

In the flow without teacher input, Visual Link helps the child identify the topic covered at school before starting the review.

2

Review the target skill

The system presents locally generated exercises within a closed curriculum universe, keeping the session close to the selected skill.

3

Use a hint

When an answer is incorrect and a hint is available, the child can ask for help and review a prompt before trying again.

4

Try again

The second chance lets the child resume the reasoning process. The Concept Library can explain a related concept separately from the hint for that specific exercise.

5

Maintain continuity

Orbit rules can temporarily reduce difficulty or recover a prerequisite, always aiming to guide the child back to the target skill.

Principle: an error is a signal to provide support and reorganize the experience, not a mechanism for punishment or competition.

Technology for autonomy

Offline-first

Curriculum content, graphs, templates, and rules remain on the device, reducing network dependence during review.

Goal: work in contexts with limited connectivity as well

TTS and accessibility

The operating system's text-to-speech can read prompts and instructions, reducing dependence on an adult reader.

Use: optional for fluent readers and relevant for children with lower reading fluency

Rule-based adaptation

The orbit uses TARGET, TARGET-1, TARGET-2, and TARGET+1. The curriculum graph is used to recover prerequisites, not to advance into untaught content.

Principle: explicit, auditable logic without semantic diagnosis of the error

For schools, families, and partners

SVEED is designed to complement the school's work and increase the child's autonomy at home, without requiring an adult to guide every exercise.

School and teacher

In teacher mode, the school can indicate the curriculum skill that should guide that day's review at home.

Child

The interface combines optional audio, hints, a second chance, and a short routine to support progressively independent use.

Family

The parent panel provides a simple session-level summary, including time, subject, exercises, errors, and hint use.

Institutional partners

The offline-first architecture and low technical execution cost support deployment scenarios on Android devices and in limited-connectivity contexts.

The goal is to add a layer of individual review after class, not to create a parallel school inside the device.

Privacy, security, and limits

SVEED describes security according to what is implemented today, without turning technical intent into a promise.

Data minimization

The review does not require the child's name, national ID number, email, or phone number in order to work.

Pseudonymization

Persistent technical identifiers and session records exist. Therefore, the accurate description is not absolute anonymity.

Offline-first operation

The educational session runs locally and does not depend on continuous connectivity to cloud services.

No generative AI during the session

Questions shown to the child are produced from local rules and templates, not from calls to generative models.

Session records

The current implementation records session-level information such as time, subject, skill, correct answers, errors, and hint use.

Explicit limits

The product does not currently claim to be tamper-proof, provide absolute anonymity, or guarantee cryptographic integrity against intentional tampering.

Responsible scope

SVEED is designed for guided individual curriculum review. In its current state, it should not be presented as a system that:

  • replaces teachers, classroom instruction, or the curriculum
  • semantically diagnoses the cause of each error
  • autonomously introduces untaught curriculum content
  • already has completed formal external validation
  • guarantees absolute anonymity or technical inviolability
