The last mile does not end at the school door

Learning has to continue after class. Homework and other forms of at-home review have long helped students practice and consolidate what was taught in school. The challenge is that the conditions needed to sustain that continuity are not equally available across households.

International organizations recognize multiple dimensions of education's last mile. The OECD notes that it can extend into students' homes and personal devices Source: OECD 2023 ; UNICEF uses the term last-mile learners for children who are being left behind and are among the hardest to reach Source: UNICEF 2022 .

Classroom instruction is collective

A teacher leads an entire class. After the lesson, each child may need to revisit different points and consolidate a different part of what was taught.

Review has to continue

The OECD recommends short, regular homework assignments. The Education Endowment Foundation (EEF) reports positive average effects of homework, with larger effects in secondary than in primary education, and highlights tasks linked to classroom learning and accompanied by feedback. OECD — PISA 2022, vol. V ↗ · EEF — Homework ↗

Home learning conditions are unequal

Time, quiet space, connectivity, devices, and academic support are not equally available. OECD data links these differences to socioeconomic gaps in homework participation Source: PISA 2018 . SVEED was designed to reduce some of these dependencies.

What SVEED is

SVEED is an Android system conceived for children in public education across the Global South. It starts in Brazil, with particular attention to families in the C, D and E socioeconomic groups, making structured, independent home review of content already taught at school possible. Exercises, hints and support when children get stuck reduce reliance on continuous connectivity and adult explanations.

SVEED — Sistema de Viabilização de Estudo Estruturado Domiciliar (a system for enabling structured study at home). Its Brazilian curriculum structure covers Years 1–9 of Brazil’s Ensino Fundamental (primary and lower secondary education): Mathematics, Portuguese, Science, History, and Geography across those years, and English in Years 6–9. Specialist educational review is at different stages across subjects and year groups.

From Brazil to other public-school curricula: the Brazilian curriculum foundation follows the BNCC. Reaching other public education systems in the Global South will require translation, alignment with each country’s curriculum, independent educational review and validation with local partners. SVEED does not yet operate outside Brazil.

Review

The starting point is a curriculum skill already covered at school, identified by the child or selected through teacher mode.

Progressive support

After an incorrect answer, the child can receive a hint and try again. Support guides the reasoning process without turning the session into a performance scoreboard.

Prerequisites

When difficulty persists, predefined rules can step back to an associated earlier skill and then return the child to the original learning path.

Product characteristics

  • Offline-first: the educational session does not depend on continuous connectivity
  • Android: content, rules, graphs, and templates remain available locally
  • No generative AI during the session: questions are produced from local rules and templates
  • No gamification: no coins, rankings, streaks, or persistent performance scoreboard during review

Schools teach. SVEED makes review possible. Educational assessments measure proficiency. The product is not intended to replace teachers, classroom instruction, or the curriculum, and it should not autonomously introduce curriculum content that has not yet been taught.

How it works

A short, predictable, individual routine designed to help the child keep going when difficulty appears.

SVEED centers on a daily review session lasting 15 minutes, regardless of how quickly the child answers. This duration provides a short, predictable routine; it has not been established as the optimal duration for learning. The interface uses a small number of controls while the system organizes the sequence, offers support, and adjusts difficulty.

1

Confirm what was studied

When no teacher has selected the topic, Nexo Visual (visual topic confirmation) helps the child identify the topic covered at school before starting the review.

2

Review the target skill

The system presents locally generated exercises within a defined set of curriculum content, keeping the session close to the selected skill.

3

Use a hint

When an answer is incorrect and a hint is available, the child can ask for help and review a prompt before trying again.

4

Try again

The second chance lets the child resume the reasoning process. The Concept Library can explain a related concept separately from the hint for that specific exercise.

5

Maintain continuity

Orbit rules can temporarily reduce difficulty or recover a prerequisite, always aiming to guide the child back to the target skill.

Principle: an error is a signal to provide support and reorganize the experience, not a mechanism for punishment or competition.

Technology for independent learning

Offline-first

Curriculum content, graphs, templates, and rules remain on the device, reducing network dependence during review.

Goal: work in contexts with limited connectivity as well

Text-to-speech and accessibility

The operating system's text-to-speech can read prompts and instructions, reducing dependence on an adult reader.

Use: optional for fluent readers and relevant for children with lower reading fluency

Rule-based adaptation

The orbit uses TARGET, TARGET-1, TARGET-2, and TARGET+1. The curriculum graph is used to recover prerequisites, not to advance into untaught content.

Principle: explicit, auditable logic without semantic diagnosis of the error

The curriculum system and the evaluation APK

The evaluation APK provides Mathematics for Years 2 and 7 as a demonstration sample. This sample does not define the scope of the full system or establish that all years and subjects have been validated.

The institutional evaluation APK has been signed with the developer’s key and undergone technical and functional testing within the current scope.

Evidence still needed

Supporting learning is SVEED’s educational aim. Research on homework informs the approach, but does not establish the app’s effectiveness. Its specific effects on the regularity of review and on learning have not yet been measured systematically; external evaluation should examine both.

Current position: a functional educational product with architecture sufficiently developed to justify evaluation and a pilot, but without a formal external outcomes study yet.

For schools, families, and partners

SVEED is designed to complement the school’s work and help children review with less need for adult explanations. Independent participation does not require children to start on their own: a parent or caregiver may prompt them to begin and help organize the routine.

School and teacher

In teacher mode, the school can indicate the curriculum skill that should guide that day's review at home.

Child

The interface combines optional audio, hints, a second chance, and a short routine to support progressively independent use.

Family

The parent dashboard provides a simple session-level summary, including time, subject, exercises, errors, and hint use.

Institutional partners

Public education systems, educators and organizations can help review the content, engage families and design a pilot. Device distribution and use verification are complementary institutional possibilities.

Partners for the education mission: we seek people and institutions committed to expanding access to school review in public education. The Android foundation and first Brazilian curriculum implementation already exist. Next come independent educational review, input from families and teachers, a documented pilot, product improvements and a sustainable operation. Privacy, security, and limits

SVEED describes security according to what is implemented today, without turning technical intent into a promise.

Data minimization

The review does not require the child's name, national ID number, email, or phone number in order to work.

Pseudonymization

Persistent technical identifiers and session records exist. Therefore, the accurate description is not absolute anonymity.

Offline-first operation

The educational session runs locally and does not depend on continuous connectivity to cloud services.

No generative AI during the session

Questions shown to the child are produced from local rules and templates, not from calls to generative models.

Session records

The current implementation records session-level information such as time, subject, skill, correct answers, errors, and hint use.

Explicit limits

The product does not currently claim to be tamper-proof, provide absolute anonymity, or guarantee cryptographic integrity against intentional tampering.

Responsible scope

SVEED is designed for guided review of school content. In its current state, it should not be presented as a system that:

  • replaces teachers, classroom instruction, or the curriculum
  • identifies the underlying reason for each incorrect answer
  • autonomously introduces untaught curriculum content
  • already has completed formal external validation
  • guarantees absolute anonymity or technical inviolability
